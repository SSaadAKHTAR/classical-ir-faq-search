Climate Change: Understanding the Global Crisis

Climate change refers to long-term shifts in temperatures and weather patterns on Earth. While climate change can occur naturally, scientific evidence shows that human activities have been the main driver of climate change since the 1800s, primarily due to the burning of fossil fuels like coal, oil, and gas.

Global warming is the long-term heating of Earth's surface observed since the pre-industrial period between 1850 and 1900. The average global temperature has increased by approximately 1.1 degrees Celsius since the late 19th century. The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) was established in 1988 by the United Nations to provide scientific assessments on climate change.

The greenhouse effect is a natural process that warms Earth's surface. When the Sun's energy reaches Earth, some of it is reflected back to space and the rest is absorbed, warming the planet. The main greenhouse gases include carbon dioxide (CO2), methane (CH4), nitrous oxide (N2O), and water vapor. Carbon dioxide accounts for about 76 percent of total greenhouse gas emissions.

Human activities that contribute to climate change include burning fossil fuels for electricity and heat, deforestation, industrial processes, and agriculture. The transportation sector is responsible for approximately 24 percent of global CO2 emissions. China is currently the world's largest emitter of greenhouse gases, followed by the United States and India.

The Paris Agreement, adopted in 2015 by 196 countries at the COP21 conference in Paris, France, aims to limit global warming to well below 2 degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels. The agreement was signed on April 22, 2016, also known as Earth Day. As of 2023, 195 parties have signed the agreement, making it one of the most widely supported international climate accords.

The impacts of climate change are already visible worldwide. Sea levels have risen by about 20 centimeters since 1901, with the rate of increase accelerating. Arctic sea ice is declining at a rate of 13 percent per decade. Extreme weather events, including hurricanes, droughts, and wildfires, are becoming more frequent and severe. The 2020 Australian bushfires burned an estimated 18.6 million hectares of land.

Renewable energy sources offer solutions to reduce greenhouse gas emissions. Solar power capacity has increased by more than 20 times over the past decade. Wind energy provided approximately 9.2 percent of global electricity generation in 2022. Denmark generates about 47 percent of its electricity from wind power, the highest percentage in the world.

Several countries and regions have committed to achieving net-zero emissions. The European Union aims to become climate-neutral by 2050. The United Kingdom has set a legally binding target to reach net zero by 2050, making it the first major economy to pass such legislation in 2019. New Zealand passed the Climate Change Response Act in 2019, committing to net-zero emissions by 2050.

Individual actions can help combat climate change, including reducing energy consumption, using public transportation, eating less meat, and supporting renewable energy. A study published in Environmental Research Letters in 2017 found that the four highest-impact actions individuals can take are having fewer children, living car-free, avoiding air travel, and eating a plant-based diet.

Scientists at NASA's Goddard Institute for Space Studies in New York monitor global temperature changes using data from weather stations, ships, and satellites. The National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) headquarters located in Silver Spring, Maryland, also plays a crucial role in climate research and monitoring.
